10.9 Attention and Transformers

Recurrence reads a sequence through a relay: token 0’s information passes through T pairs of hands before reaching the output. Attention removes the relay — every position reads every other position directly, with content-addressed weights computed on the fly. Stacked with positional encodings, residuals, and feedforward blocks, it becomes the transformer: the architecture of modern sequence models (and of Part 13’s LLMs). This page computes attention by hand in NumPy and measures the properties the equations claim.

Tier 3 — transformer training runs in Colab; the attention arithmetic below runs live:

1. Basic Concept

Scaled dot-product attention: given queries \(Q\) (what I’m looking for), keys \(K\) (what I contain), values \(V\) (what I’ll hand over):

\[\mathrm{Attn}(Q, K, V) = \mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) V\]

Each output token is a weighted average of all values, weights = softmaxed similarities between its query and all keys. Self-attention sets \(Q = XW_Q\), \(K = XW_K\), \(V = XW_V\) — the sequence attends to itself. Three properties define the architecture:

  1. O(1) path length between any two positions (vs recurrence’s O(T)) — long-range dependencies are one hop (10.7/10.8’s disease, cured structurally).
  2. Permutation equivariance — attention is a set operation; order enters only through added positional encodings.
  3. Parallelism — no sequential state; all positions compute at once (GPUs breathe).

A transformer block: x = x + Attn(LN(x)); x = x + FFN(LN(x)) — attention mixes across positions, the feedforward processes each position, residuals keep paths short. Multi-head attention runs h of these in parallel subspaces and concatenates.

2. Concept Questions

  1. Why divide by \(\sqrt{d_k}\)? Compute (mentally, then in the drills) the scale of \(q \cdot k\) for random vectors as \(d_k\) grows, and what an unnormalized softmax does to the gradient.
  2. Attention is permutation-equivariant: if you shuffle the input tokens, exactly what happens to the outputs? What breaks the symmetry, and why is “add position” better than “shuffle order into the weights”?
  3. The causal mask adds \(-10^9\) to scores above the diagonal before the softmax. What does the mask accomplish after softmax (numerically), and why not multiply attention weights by zero after?
  4. Multi-head splits \(d\) into h subspaces of \(d_k = d/h\). What does a single head cannot represent that heads can, in one sentence about positions vs channels?
  5. Self-attention on a length-T sequence costs O(T²·d). Name the two standard mitigations and the length regime where each is the obvious choice.
Answers
  1. For random \(d_k\)-dim vectors, \(q\cdot k \sim \mathcal{N}(0, d_k)\) — its scale grows as \(\sqrt{d_k}\). Raw scores of ±10 make softmax near-one-hot: gradients through the softmax vanish (only the argmax key receives gradient flow), heads stop learning. Dividing by \(\sqrt{d_k}\) keeps score variance ≈ 1 → diffuse, trainable attention.
  2. Outputs are the same permutation of the permuted computation: token “cat” gets exactly the output it would have gotten elsewhere. Order enters via positional encodings added to embeddings — better than baking order into weights because (i) relative/interpolable positions, (ii) weights stay position-agnostic (transfer across lengths), (iii) equivariance keeps the operator simple and analyzable.
  3. \(e^{-10^9} = 0\) after exponentiation (in float32, underflow to exactly 0): masked positions get identically-zero weight and zero gradient. Zeroing after softmax breaks row normalization (remaining weights sum < 1 — the output silently shrinks toward nothing for early tokens) and still leaks gradient into masked scores.
  4. One head averages over the single similarity pattern it computes; it cannot attend to “position-2-when-channel-says-verb” and “last-position-when-channel-says-subject” simultaneously. Heads = parallel, independent attention patterns over different subspaces — multiple “reading strategies” per layer.
  5. Sparse/local attention (windowed, sliding) for long documents with local structure; linear/low-rank approximations (or hierarchical/chunking) when full global mixing is unnecessary. Plain full attention: fine to a few thousand tokens; the KV cache (Part 13’s inference concern) is the memory wall.

3. Drills & Exercises

Drills

Predict, then run.

Exercises (with solutions)

Exercise 1. Implement attention(Q, K, V, mask=None) in NumPy with the scale, the causal mask, and stable softmax. Verify: (a) rows sum to 1 with and without mask; (b) masked entries are exactly 0; (c) with \(Q = K = V\) fixed to orthonormal-ish rows, the output at position 0 equals \(v_0\) (a token with nothing behind it and a self-aligned query attends only to itself). Then break it two ways — remove the scale, apply the mask after softmax — and report the two symptoms.

Solution
Symptoms: without the scale, entropy collapses (softmax saturates — gradients die, learning stops); masking after softmax leaves rows summing to ~0.3–0.7 — every early token’s output shrinks toward zero, a bug that looks like “the model is faint”. The discipline: scale → mask → softmax → matmul, in that order.

Exercise 2. Positional encodings, seen and used: implement the sinusoidal encoding \(\mathrm{PE}_{(pos, 2i)} = \sin(pos/10000^{2i/d})\), \(\mathrm{PE}_{(pos, 2i+1)} = \cos(\ldots)\) for T = 64, d = 32; plot it. Then demonstrate why: build the attention of Section 1 on shuffled tokens — with and without positional encodings added — and show that only the encoded version’s outputs change under shuffling (equivariance broken on purpose). At what frequency do position 5 and 6 become hardest to distinguish?

Solution
The encoding is a set of sinusoids across wavelengths: near positions differ in high frequencies (rapid oscillation across dimension), far positions in low ones. Without PE, the shuffle changes nothing (max deviation = 0 — pure equivariance); with PE, shuffling inputs permutes positions of codes, and attention outputs differ — order is now represented. High-frequency dimensions distinguish adjacent positions best; the lowest frequencies are nearly identical for 5 vs 6 (their information lives in the fast dims). Learned positional embeddings do the same job with more flexibility and zero interpretability.

Exercise 3. The quadratic wall, plotted: for T ∈ {64, 256, 1024, 4096}, compute the shapes (not the contents) of all intermediate attention matrices and report memory (elements) for scores, weights, and output at d = 64. Then compare against a bidirectional LSTM’s state memory (2·h per step, h = 128). At what T does attention’s score matrix alone exceed the LSTM’s total activation memory, and what does that predict about “long-context” engineering (Part 13’s KV-cache problem, sparsity, sliding windows)?

Solution
The T×T matrices dwarf everything: at T = 4096 the scores alone are 16.7M elements (67 MB in float32) per head per layer — while the LSTM’s sequential activations are 1M. Crossing point sits near T ≈ 2·h = 256 here. This table is the business case for sliding-window/sparse attention (cap T×T), KV-cache quantization (Part 13’s inference stack), and why “context length” is a product spec, not a detail. Note what is not the problem: the O(T·d) output and the weights’ compute — it’s the all-pairs score matrix.

4. Advanced Information & Pitfalls

  • Order of operations is law: scale → mask → softmax → @V. Applying the mask after softmax breaks normalization; applying softmax over the wrong axis (normalize columns instead of rows) produces a valid-looking matrix that is semantically “which queries attend to me” — a silent role reversal.
  • Attention weights ≠ explanations: a high weight means the value was used, not why; multiple heads and layers distribute credit, and gradient-based attribution often disagrees with weight inspection. Treat attention maps as telemetry, not causality.
  • Residuals and layer norm are structural, not decorative: without the residual, deep stacks lose the gradient path (depth 12+ untrainable); pre-LN (x + Attn(LN(x))) is the modern default for stable deep training.
  • Causal vs padding masks compose: batched inference with variable lengths needs both (future positions and padded positions); forgetting the padding mask lets real tokens attend to zeros-as-content.
  • The KV cache (inference): self-attention recomputes keys/values for all past tokens each step unless cached — O(T) memory per sequence is the deployment constraint behind Part 13’s serving discussions.
  • Label leakage by masking bug: a wrongly-oriented causal mask (attending forward) trains a model that sees the answer — perplexity looks spectacular, the model is useless. Always unit-test the mask on a tiny T.

Read & Refactor AI Code

This AI-generated self-attention has three bugs. Diagnose before opening the fix.

What’s wrong, and the fix
  1. No \(\sqrt{d_k}\) scale: scores have std ≈ \(\sqrt{d} \approx 2.8\) — softmax starts saturated; attention concentrates before training even begins.
  2. Mask by zeroing scores: scores * (1 - mask) sets future scores to 0 — which softmax turns into weight \(e^0\)/Σ, i.e., future tokens still receive attention (a strong uniform baseline, not a block). The mask must subtract −1e9 (before softmax).
  3. Softmax over axis 0: normalizes columns — “how much is this key attended across queries” — so each query’s weights no longer sum to 1 and the output is an unnormalized mishmash. Rows (axis −1).

5. Advanced Questions

Q1. Prove attention’s permutation equivariance formally: for a permutation matrix \(P\), show \(\mathrm{Attn}(PX) = P\,\mathrm{Attn}(X)\) (with shared \(W_Q, W_K, W_V\)), and that adding positional encodings breaks it in a controlled way: $(PX + PE’) $ where \(PE'\) is the encoding of permuted positions — equals \(P\) times the original iff the encoding itself is transported consistently. Conclude: why “attention = set operation + position injection” is the right mental model (and what it predicts about length generalization when encodings are sinusoidal vs learned).

Hint Permutation matrices are orthogonal: \(P^\top P = I\). Push \(P\) through \(QK^\top\) using \((PX)(PX)^\top = P(X X^\top)P^\top\), then watch the softmax commute with row permutation.
Solution With \(Q' = PXW_Q\): \(Q'K'^\top = P X W_Q W_K^\top X^\top P^\top = P S P^\top\) (scores permuted both ways). Softmax normalizes rows → \(A' = P A P^\top\). Then \(A' V' = P A P^\top P V = P A V\). QED — outputs permute with inputs. With encodings: attention reads \(PX + PE_{\pi}\); the output equals \(P\,\mathrm{Attn}(X + PE)\) iff \(PE_{\pi} = P\,PE\) — i.e., positions were permuted with tokens. Prediction: sinusoidal encodings (a fixed function of index) transport exactly; learned position embeddings transport only for indices seen in training — hence transformers with learned positions generalize poorly beyond trained lengths, while sinusoidal/relative encodings extrapolate (imperfectly — RoPE-style relative schemes exist precisely because absolute transport still degrades).

Q2. One head, one convex set — a capacity argument: every attention head’s output at row \(t\) is a convex combination of the values (softmax weights are nonnegative, sum to 1). Construct (in NumPy) a target that is not in the convex hull of the values — e.g., the token-difference signal \(v_t - v_{t-1}\) — and show (a) that no single head can produce it (search many random heads; then argue via the hull), while (b) two heads — one attending to self, one to the predecessor — combined with a signed linear mixer (\(+1\), \(-1\)) reproduce it exactly. What does this say about multi-head as expressive range, not just parallel compute?

Hint Differences need negative coefficients; convex weights forbid them. Identity and shift matrices are perfectly realizable attention patterns (softmax of suitable logits).
Solution
One softmax per row yields weights \(a \ge 0\), \(\sum a = 1\): the head’s output rows live in the convex hull of the values — they can average tokens but never subtract them. \(v_t - v_{t-1}\) requires a coefficient of \(-1\): unreachable by any head, however trained (the 4000-head search plateaus away from 0). Two heads with a signed mixing layer reach it exactly — and both attention patterns used (identity, shift) are ordinary softmax outputs of suitable logit matrices. The general lesson: heads widen what a layer can represent (each contributes its own convex set; the mixer takes signed combinations), not just how much compute runs in parallel.

6. Complex Questions

C1 (× 10.6 convolutions × 10.8). Three priors, one table: locality (conv), ordering-through-state (RNN), content-addressing (attention). For each of (a) 224×224 images, (b) 2k-token documents, (c) a 10⁶-step sensor stream, (d) a 20-sample clinical table — argue the best-matched inductive bias and the cost of the mismatch in both directions. Then write the two-sentence version of why modern architectures are hybrids (patch-embed + attention; conv stem + transformer; state-space models as the recurrence-attention compromise).

C2 (× Part 13 concepts × 10.4). Attention removed the relay; the transformer kept everything else from the deep-learning playbook — including 10.4‘s optimizer reality. Explain, with the specific mechanisms named, why transformers are more sensitive to learning-rate warmup than MLPs: (i) layer-norm’s gradient amplification early in training, (ii) attention logits’ softmax saturation at bad init (the \(\sqrt{d_k}\) story), (iii) residual-stream growth over depth. Then predict — and check in the Colab lab — which of the three failures (divergence, plateau, attention collapse) you see with warmup removed.

7. Summary

  • Attention = content-addressed weighted averaging; \(\mathrm{softmax}(QK^\top/\sqrt{d_k})V\) — every position reaches every other in one hop.
  • Scale by \(\sqrt{d_k}\), mask before softmax (subtract −1e9), normalize over the query axis — the order is law.
  • Attention alone is a set operation; positional encodings inject order; sinusoidal transports, learned doesn’t extrapolate.
  • Multi-head = parallel reading strategies; single heads cannot hold two incompatible attention distributions.
  • The O(T²) score matrix is the architecture’s wall — sparse/Windows/cache engineering (Part 13) exists because of this table.
  • Blocks = attention (mix) + FFN (process) + residuals/LN (trainability); transformers are 10.4’s optimizer physics at scale.
  • You now know the machinery; the last question is judgment — 10.10 when deep learning is appropriate.
