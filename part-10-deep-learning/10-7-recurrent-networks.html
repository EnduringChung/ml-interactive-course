10.7 Recurrent Neural Networks

Sequences break fixed-size models: sentences, sensor traces, and gene windows have variable length, and order carries meaning (the lag features of 2.8 hard-code one horizon). A recurrent network processes a sequence with the same weights at every step, carrying a hidden state forward: \(h_t = \tanh(W_h h_{t-1} + W_x x_t + b)\). This page builds the recurrence, unrolls it, and watches its gradients vanish — all live in NumPy.

Tier 3 — RNN training at scale runs in Colab; the recurrence and its gradients are small enough to run live:

1. Basic Concept

\[h_t = g(W_h h_{t-1} + W_x x_t + b), \qquad \hat y_t = W_y h_t + c\]

  • \(h_t\) is a fixed-size summary of everything seen so far — the network’s memory. Same \(W_h, W_x, W_y\) at every \(t\): parameter count is independent of sequence length (weight sharing through time — the sequence cousin of 10.6’s spatial sharing).
  • Unrolling: for a length-T sequence, the recurrence is a T-layer deep network with tied weights. Training = backpropagation through time (BPTT): unroll, backprop as usual (10.4), sum parameter gradients over t.
  • The catch is visible in the chain rule: \(\partial h_T / \partial h_t\) is a product of T−t Jacobians (\(D_h = \mathrm{diag}(1 - h^2) W_h\) per step) — products of norms < 1 vanish exponentially (long-range dependencies unlearnable), > 1 explode (NaN).

Worked example — a vanilla RNN’s hidden state as a running summary, live:

Each hidden unit is a smoothed, delayed response to the input’s history — the raw material a trained output layer would read predictions from.

2. Concept Questions

  1. Weight sharing through time vs through space (10.6): what does each buy, and why does variable-length handling come free only in the recurrent case?
  2. \(\partial h_T/\partial h_t = \prod_{s=t+1}^{T} D_h(s) W_h\). Why can’t the diagonal tanh factors rescue you when \(\|W_h\| \approx 1\), even though tanh’ ≤ 1 only mildly shrinks?
  3. An RNN trained on sentences of length 5–15 fails to use information from position 1 when predicting position 40. Name the mathematical diagnosis, and the three standard engineering responses.
  4. Why must the hidden state be reset between independent sequences in a batch, and what bug does a forgotten reset produce that looks like “the model sometimes works”?
  5. Truncated BPTT (backprop through only the last k steps) trades what for what, and why is it usually a good trade for long streams?
Answers
  1. Spatial sharing buys translation equivariance over a grid; temporal sharing buys composition over time — the same “detector of the recent past” applies at every t. Variable length: a conv net’s output size grows with input width (a grid), while an RNN’s hidden state is a fixed point you can read at any t — one model, any length.
  2. The tanh’ factors bound the shrink at 1 but typically sit near 0.2–0.5 (tanh output often ≈ 0.5–0.9 away from saturation ⇒ slope 0.2–0.7); multiplied by \(\|W_h\| \approx 1\), the per-step factor is < 1 for most steps — over 30+ steps even 0.8 per step is \(0.8^{30} \approx 10^{-3}\). Rescue requires the product of per-step factors ≈ 1 consistently — measure-zero lucky alignment (this motivates 10.8’s additive cell).
  3. Vanishing gradient through time. Responses: (i) gate the recurrence (LSTM/GRU, 10.8); (ii) gradient clipping for the exploding side; (iii) architectural replacement — attention (10.9) gives a direct O(1) path between any two positions.
  4. \(h_0\) of the next sequence otherwise starts from the last state of an unrelated one — the first predictions are contaminated by a “memory” of different data. Symptom: batch-dependent, order-dependent accuracy that shuffles when you reorder the dataloader.
  5. It truncates the dependency graph at k steps: gradients from events older than k never flow. Trades long-range credit assignment for O(k) memory and compute per step, plus a shorter, better-conditioned product in the chain rule — for streams where most signal is local, k ≈ 20–200 loses little.

3. Drills & Exercises

Drills

Predict, then run.

Exercises (with solutions)

Exercise 1. Train a vanilla RNN (NumPy, full BPTT) to predict the next value of a noisy sine from the previous 20 steps: 1 input → 8 hidden (tanh) → 1 output, MSE. Keep the implementation explicit — forward storing all \(h_t\), backward accumulating \(\partial L/\partial W_h\) over t — and gradient-check one weight entry against finite differences. Plot loss and prediction-vs-truth.

Solution
The backward loop is Exercise-1 of 10.4 unrolled in time: dh is the upstream gradient, dtanh applies the local slope, and dWh += hs[t-1].T @ dtanh accumulates the same matrix’s gradient across t — weight sharing means summing, exactly as convolutions sum over spatial positions. (The trained MSE ≈ 0.03 lags the lag-window baselines of Exercise 3 — on a fixed-horizon sine, the simple model is hard to beat.)

Exercise 2. The vanishing gradient, quantified end-to-end: using Exercise 1’s (untrained) network, feed a unit impulse at t = 0 and measure \(\|\partial h_T / \partial h_0\|\) for T ∈ {5, 10, 20, 40} with \(W_h\) scaled to spectral radius ∈ {0.5, 0.9, 0.99, 1.05}. Plot log-magnitude vs T for each radius. Then compute the slope of each line and relate it to the spectral radius — the per-step gradient factor. What radius keeps a 100-step gradient alive above \(10^{-2}\)?

Solution
Log-log lines with slope ≈ log(per-step factor): radius 0.5 → slope ≈ −1.4 (dead by T = 20); 0.9 → slope ≈ −0.2; 0.99 → ≈ flat-to-exploding variance; 1.05 → exponential growth. To keep \(\|J\| > 10^{-2}\) at T = 100 you need per-step > 0.955, i.e., ρ within ~5% of 1 and tanh staying near-linear — a knife’s edge. This is the theorem-level reason vanilla RNNs learn only local structure and 10.8 exists.

Exercise 3. Recurrence vs lag-windowing: on the sine task of Exercise 1, compare the RNN against an ordinary linear regression and a small MLP on fixed lag features (the last K = 20 values, 2.8-style). Same data, same split. Which wins, by how much, and what changes if the series is length-variable (chunks of 10–50 steps)? State the honest scope of “RNNs handle variable length”.

Solution
On fixed-horizon, local tasks, lag features + a shallow model match the RNN — recurrence is not magic, it’s weight-tied feature extraction over a window (2.8 with learned weighting). The RNN’s honest advantage is (i) parameter count independent of K (K = 500 lags = 500× more linear weights, the RNN unchanged), (ii) state that persists across chunk boundaries within a stream, (iii) inputs of genuinely variable length without padding/imputation hacks. If your problem has a fixed, short horizon: window it and use the simpler model — 10.10’s rule.

4. Advanced Information & Pitfalls

  • Clip the global norm, not per-coordinate: clip_grad_norm_ rescales the whole gradient vector when its total norm exceeds a threshold (typically 1–5); per-coordinate clipping distorts direction. Without clipping, one exploding step throws the parameters into a bad region it takes epochs to re-enter.
  • Stateful vs stateless training: reset \(h_0 = 0\) per (shuffled) sample for independent sequences; carry state across consecutive chunks of the same long stream — mixing these two modes is the classic silent contamination bug.
  • Batch-first vs time-first: PyTorch’s nn.RNN default input is (seq, batch, feature) — batch_first=True changes it; a transposition bug trains “fine” while shuffling unrelated sequences’ timesteps.
  • Output-at-every-step vs last-step: sequence labelling (POS tags) reads \(\hat y_t\) at each t; sequence classification reads only \(h_T\). Using the wrong head halves your supervision or fabricates per-step targets.
  • Deep RNNs stack states, not weights: layer 2’s input is layer 1’s \(h_t\) sequence — each layer still shares its weights through time; depth adds abstraction, not memory length (10.8/10.9 address length).
  • Init matters more than in feedforward nets: \(W_h\) initialized to orthogonal matrices (spectral radius ≈ 1) keeps gradients alive longer; IRNN (identity init) is the ReLU-era equivalent.

Read & Refactor AI Code

This AI-generated BPTT training loop has three bugs. Diagnose before opening the fix.

What’s wrong, and the fix
  1. State carried across independent passes: h_state persists between epochs (and would between unrelated sequences in a batch) — the model appears to “warm up” nondeterministically; reset per sequence.
  2. Fragile gradient plumbing: dh = Wy * 2*(yhat - y) happens to work element-wise here only because the shapes collapse; in the general (h,)@(h,1) case it’s a transpose error waiting — compute err = 2*(yhat - y) then dh = Wy * err with explicit shapes, and gradient-check once (10.4).
  3. No weight is ever updated: dWh/dWx are never accumulated and nothing is subtracted from any parameter — the loop computes gradients into the void, prints them, and the “model” is a fixed random feature extractor forever. It “runs” and prints plausible numbers — the most dangerous failure mode in a hand-rolled trainer.

5. Advanced Questions

Q1. Derive the exact gradient \(\partial L / \partial W_h\) for the vanilla RNN over a length-T sequence with loss read only at the final step, in terms of the per-step Jacobians \(J_t = D_t W_h\). Show it is a sum over “paths” and that each path is a matrix product — then prove the norm bound \(\|\partial h_T/\partial h_t\| \le \prod_s \|J_s\|\) and its LTR (lower-triangular radius) refinement. At what distribution of per-step factors does the bound become loose enough to matter?

Hint Unroll: \(h_T\) is a composition of T maps; chain rule gives the product; submultiplicativity gives the bound. The bound ignores cancellation between large factors with different directions.
Solution \(\frac{\partial L}{\partial W_h} = \sum_{t=1}^{T} \frac{\partial L}{\partial h_T} \left(\prod_{s=t+1}^{T} J_s\right) \frac{\partial h_t}{\partial W_h}\), where \(\partial h_t / \partial W_h\) picks the outer product \(D_t \, h_{t-1}^\top\)-like term (\(W_h\) appears only in step t’s map). Each bracket is a product of T−t Jacobians; by submultiplicativity \(\|\prod J_s\| \le \prod \|J_s\|\) — the vanishing/exploding bound. The bound is tight when factors align (stable recurrent dynamics have a dominant direction, so they often are nearly tight); it is loose when factors alternate directions and cancel — numerically, gradients near the “edge of chaos” (\(\rho \approx 1\)) show large log-variance across t and across seeds, so average behavior is better than the worst-case bound but reliability is worse. Design consequence: don’t engineer for the bound’s tightness — engineer the per-step factor itself (10.8’s constant-error carousel or 10.9’s O(1) paths).

Q2. Echo-state property, made empirical: an untrained RNN with \(W_h\) scaled to spectral radius ρ is a reservoir; train only the readout \(W_y\) (ridge on collected hidden states). The task must demand memory: delayed recall — each 100-step window’s target is the value at its start, so the state must hold one number for 100 steps. Sweep ρ ∈ {0.3, 0.6, 0.8, 0.95, 1.1} and report test MSE. Explain the optimum’s location (edge of chaos) and what happens on each side: why does small ρ fail structurally here, and what does the readout forgive?

Hint Too small ρ: all states collapse to a static map of the recent input (no memory of the window’s start). Too large: chaos — states are informative but unreadable. The ridge readout forgives a lot.
Solution
MSE improves steadily toward ρ ≈ 0.95 and ticks up at 1.1: below the edge of chaos the recurrence is contractive — 100 multiplications by ‖J‖ < 1 erase the window’s start, and no readout can recover what the state discarded (the failure is structural); near ρ = 1 the state is a near-isometric carrier of history, and ridge decodes it; past it, chaos adds variance faster than memory. Reservoir computing is the untrained-weight-limit of the RNN — a useful mental model: recurrent dynamics provide features; the readout does the fitting; training \(W_h\) (BPTT) moves those features toward the task, which is exactly what the vanishing gradient makes hard (10.8 re-engineers the per-step factor instead).

6. Complex Questions

C1 (× 10.9 attention × 10.5 gradient clipping). Two cures for the same disease: gradient clipping (treats exploding only) and gated cells (treats vanishing). Construct on the length-40 task a regime where clipping is necessary but not sufficient (large-init \(W_h\): watch the loss spike-then-plateau with clipping alone), and one where gating is necessary (long-range copy task). Then argue — with the two experiments as evidence — why the field moved to attention rather than better clipping schedules.

C2 (× 11.1 why survival data is special × 6.4 regression splines). A clinical time series (vitals every hour, censored follow-up) is proposed for an RNN-based risk model. Write the design memo: which components of the RNN pipeline (state reset, truncation length, output head, loss) interact with censoring and irregular sampling, which classical alternative (splines on time since baseline, Cox with time-varying covariates) is the honest baseline, and what evidence would justify the RNN. (Deliberately cross-part: the answer is mostly 11.5, not PyTorch.)

7. Summary

  • RNN = same cell applied through time; hidden state is a fixed-size running summary; parameters independent of length.
  • Training unrolls to a deep net (BPTT); the same weight matrix multiplies the gradient every step.
  • Products of Jacobians ⇒ vanishing (ρ < 1) or exploding (ρ > 1) gradients; clipping treats only the explosive half.
  • Reset state between independent sequences; truncation length is an honest, declared approximation.
  • On fixed short horizons, lag-window + shallow models match RNNs — reach for recurrence when length varies or context is long.
  • The per-step factor is the disease; gates re-engineer it — 10.8 LSTM and GRU.
