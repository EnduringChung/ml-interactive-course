3.7 Classification Model Comparison

You now own five classifiers (logistic, LDA, QDA, Naive Bayes, KNN). Choosing between them is where careless pipelines go to lie to themselves: comparing on one split, one metric, or — worst — the metric that was also used to tune. This page builds the honest comparison protocol: same folds, paired tests, calibration checks, and decision-aware selection.

Tier 1 — every cell below runs live.

1. Basic Concept

Three principles of fair comparison:

  1. Same folds, paired evaluation — models see identical train/test partitions; differences are then within-fold, far less noisy than independent estimates.
  2. Metrics match the decision — AUC/AP for ranking, calibration for probabilities, operating-point metrics for budgets (3.1, 3.1.5).
  3. Uncertainty on differences, not just points — a 0.01 AUC gap between two models on one split is noise until tested.

2. Concept Questions

  1. Why is a paired comparison (same folds) so much more powerful than comparing two independent 5-fold means?
  2. Two models have AUC 0.81 and 0.83. List, in order, the three facts you need before believing the second is better.
  3. What does a model above the diagonal in a reliability diagram (observed > predicted) indicate, and name one common pipeline step that causes systematic miscalibration.
  4. Why can Naive Bayes have a worse Brier score but nearly identical AUC to logistic — and when would you still ship NB?
  5. You tuned model A’s hyperparameters by CV and want to compare against untuned model B. What does honest comparison require?
Answers
  1. Fold-to-fold difficulty dominates variance (easy folds flatter everyone). Pairing differences out that shared noise: Var(diff) = Var(A) + Var(B) − 2Cov(A,B), and the fold-correlation makes the covariance term large — paired tests see the signal that independent-mean comparisons drown in. The same logic as paired t-tests (1.4).
    1. Uncertainty: is 0.02 within fold/seed noise (paired fold-wise test)? (2) Same preprocessing/tuning budget for both? (3) Does the metric match the deployment question — and does the gap replicate on a held-out set or fresh data?
  3. Observed frequency exceeds predicted probability → the model is systematically underconfident in that region. Causes: training on resampled/oversampled data (case–control sampling shifts the intercept — 3.3 C2); regularization that shrinks logits toward the base rate in the wrong direction; score warping in NB under feature correlation.
  4. Brier scores squared-error penalties punish NB’s overconfident probabilities (3.4); AUC, being rank-based, is blind to that inflation. Ship NB when you consume rankings (top-k retrieval) and cost nothing by probability errors — its speed and small-sample stability then win.
  5. Either tune B with the identical CV protocol (same folds/grid budget) or evaluate both with an outer CV layer untouched by tuning — nested evaluation (4.6). Comparing a tuned model’s selection-optimized number against an untuned model’s plain number conflates selection luck with model quality.

Drills

Exercises (with solutions)

Exercise 1. Build compare_models(models, X, y, k=5, seed=0) returning a DataFrame of mean±SD accuracy, AUC, AP plus pairwise McNemar p-values on pooled out-of-fold predictions. Run it on the five classifiers of Section 1; identify (a) the statistical leader, (b) the pair whose difference is not significant, (c) which metric(s) disagree with accuracy’s ranking.

Solution
Caveat carried forward: pooled out-of-fold predictions are not fully independent across folds for the McNemar counts (models refit per fold), but the paired structure is preserved — for publication-grade claims, repeat across outer resamples (4.3). The table + test pattern is the deliverable regardless.

Exercise 2. Calibration repair: the NB probabilities from Section 1’s reliability data are inflated. Implement a simple Platt-style fix: fit a 1-D logistic regression mapping NB’s logit-scored output to the true labels on a validation split, apply to test. Show Brier before/after and the reliability diagrams side by side. Confirm AUC is unchanged (rank-preserving map) — then state when this fix is not legitimate (which data it must not touch).

Solution
The calibration map is fit on a dedicated split that played no role in training the NB — touching the test set (or the original training set) for calibration would re-introduce optimism (0.5). AUC is invariant (monotone map); Brier collapses toward the logistic’s. This split discipline — fit, calibrate, test on three disjoint samples — is the production-grade pattern.

Exercise 3. Decision-theoretic selection: two models, fixed budget of 100 follow-ups per 5,000 screened (1% prevalence). Compute for each model the expected number of true hits in its top-100 (per 10 bootstrap resamples of the screening population), with a CI on the difference. Also compute each model’s precision@100. Which model wins the deployment question, and does it match the AUC winner?

Solution
The bootstrap CI on hits-in-top-100 is the deployment metric with uncertainty attached. If it excludes 0, the decision is made regardless of what AUC says — models with similar AUC can differ sharply in the head of the ranking, and budgets only ever buy the head.

4. Advanced Information & Pitfalls

  • The comparison protocol IS the result: same folds, same preprocessing (pipelines), same tuning budget, paired tests, uncertainty on differences. Deviating from any of these resets the table to anecdote.
  • Pooled out-of-fold predictions (Exercise 1) preserve pairing but fold-refitting leaks mild dependence across folds — fine for exploration; use repeated outer resampling for claims.
  • McNemar’s exact test is binomial on discordant pairs — b and c only; concordant pairs carry no comparison information. Low discordance = low power, whatever the accuracies say.
  • Brier vs AUC rank: fixing calibration never changes AUC (monotone maps) — which is why “we recalibrated and AUC didn’t move” is expected, not suspicious.
  • Multiple models ⇒ multiple comparisons: 5 models = 10 pairwise tests; control family-wise error (Bonferroni ×10) or pre-specify the primary comparison (12.3–12.4 preview).
  • Selection on the comparison metric: if the winner’s metric then becomes the reported performance, the number is selection-inflated — the nested-CV discipline of 4.6 applies with full force.

5. Advanced Questions

Q1. The “no free lunch” caveat says no classifier dominates universally. Construct two 2-D problems — one where logistic beats KNN decisively, one where KNN beats logistic — with identical n and noise. Report both test-accuracy tables and explain each winner’s edge in terms of assumption alignment (linear boundary vs local structure). What legitimate conclusion is possible about “the best classifier” for a new problem before looking at its data?

Solution
Linear truth: logistic’s inductive bias matches the boundary — it wins. Spiral: the boundary is wildly nonlinear; KNN’s local votes trace it while logistic caps out at linear. Conclusion about a new problem, pre-data: none about which wins — only about the procedure (honest CV over a small, diverse candidate set) that finds out. Model choice is an empirical property of the problem, which is why Part 4’s machinery exists.

Q2. You must compare k=7-KNN and logistic and report a p-value for “A is better.” Walk through the construction of a paired, replicated comparison: (a) why fold-wise paired t on 5 folds is weak (df = 4, dependence), (b) how repeated 5×2-fold CV (or 20 random 80/20 replications) restores power, (c) what exactly the unit of analysis is (a replication’s difference), and (d) the multiplicity caveat if 8 models enter the same protocol. Implement (b) for two models and report the CI for the accuracy difference.

Solution
  1. 5-fold paired t has df = 4 and folds share training rows — dependence inflates the nominal Type I error (the famous Dietterich point). (b) Each replication is a fresh split: differences are far less correlated across replications; 40 replications give a stable CI. (c) The unit is the replication-level difference — treat replications as the sample. (d) Eight models ⇒ 28 pairwise CIs; multiplicity control (Bonferroni/Holm) or a pre-registered primary comparison keeps the family-wise error honest (12.3 preview).

6. Complex Questions

C1 (comparison × 0.5 splitting × 4.3 CV). Build the full “league table” experiment: 6 classifiers × 20 repeated stratified 5-fold CVs, recording accuracy, AUC, and Brier per repeat. Produce (a) a ranked mean table with per-model CIs, (b) a paired heatmap of p-values (all pairs), (c) a stability statement — which model’s variance across repeats is worst and what does that imply for reproducibility of a published winner? This deliverable is the reusable template for every future model-selection question in the course.

C2 (comparison × 3.1.5 AUPRC × 1.3 Bayes). Two hospitals each evaluate your two models; hospital A has 2% disease prevalence, hospital B has 25%. Show empirically that model rankings can flip by metric family (AUC stable, AP and precision@budget flip or compress) across the two prevalences, then write the reporting paragraph that tells both hospitals which model to deploy and why — in language that survives their different baselines.

7. Summary

  • Compare models on identical folds, paired; differences need uncertainty, points don’t settle arguments.
  • McNemar for hard labels; paired fold differences for scores; replicate for power.
  • Rank metrics (AUC/AP), probability metrics (Brier, calibration), and operating-point metrics answer different questions — pick per decision, report several.
  • Calibration is a separate axis: Platt/isotonic fixes on dedicated data; AUC is blind to it, Brier isn’t.
  • Same tuning budget for every candidate; selection metrics must never double as reported performance.
  • With several candidates, multiple-comparisons discipline applies — previewing Part 12.
  • Comparison done honestly is model selection’s foundation: Part 4 formalizes the machinery — starting from why held-out error is the only currency: 4.1 The generalization problem.
