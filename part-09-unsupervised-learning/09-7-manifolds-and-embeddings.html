9.7 Manifolds, t-SNE, UMAP & Autoencoders

9.2 found linear directions of maximum variance. That is the right tool when the data really is a linear shadow of a lower-dimensional truth — and the wrong tool the moment it is not. PCA cannot find the Swiss roll’s axis no matter how many components you give it, because the roll’s geometry is curved: no straight line in R³ unrolls it. Everything on this page replaces “maximize variance along a straight line” with “preserve the neighbourhood structure,” and every method pays for it differently. The honest summary at the end is that none of these embeddings are maps of your data — they are claims about it.

Tier 3 — UMAP and the autoencoder lab need packages Pyodide does not bundle, so they run in Colab. Everything else below is live, because scikit-learn’s manifold module is wasm-available:

1. Basic Concept

The manifold premise. Data that lives in R^p but concentrates near a lower-dimensional surface of intrinsic dimension d ≪ p. The surface is curved, so no linear projection can find it — but distances along the surface still mean something. Isomap approximates that with geodesic (shortest-path-along-the-surface) distances and then finds the flat layout that reproduces them, which is classical MDS (9.3’s matrix-completion machinery in disguise).

Worked example — a Swiss roll, where PCA provably fails and Isomap does not:

Isomap’s embedding-neighbours share 0.737 of the roll coordinate on average; PCA’s share 1.643 — 2.2× worse, even though PCA is optimizing a criterion (maximum variance) that is genuinely achieved. The failure is not optimization, it is specification: variance is the wrong objective for a curved surface.

t-SNE takes a different route entirely. It does not preserve distances at all. It matches, for each point, the distribution of distances to its neighbours (a “similarity” \(P_{j|i}\), softened by a bandwidth) against a target low-dimensional distribution \(Q\) that is a Student-t — chosen precisely so the objective is cheap. The loss is a KL divergence, and the whole game is the perplexity parameter, which fixes that bandwidth as \(\exp(\text{perplexity})\) effective neighbours.

  • Unlike Isomap/MDS, t-SNE optimizes a global KL and lets local neighbourhoods dominate. Good at revealing fine structure; bad at telling you how big anything is.
  • UMAP is a close cousin with a different objective — it optimizes cross-entropy against a fuzzy set union rather than KL against a Student-t, which makes it faster and gives more globally-coherent layouts. It needs umap-learn, which Pyodide does not bundle → the Colab lab.
  • An autoencoder attacks the problem with a network instead of a distance: encode \(x \to h\) through a bottleneck, decode \(\hat x\) from \(h\), and train to minimize reconstruction error. The bottleneck is what forces a low-dimensional representation. With a linear map, a linear activation, and squared error, the optimal solution is exactly PCA — provable, and demonstrable live:

The principal cosines are 0.997 and 0.990 — the network found PCA’s 2-D subspace, found it without being told what PCA was, purely from a reconstruction objective. (The MSE has not fully converged, 0.1012 vs 0.0925: gradient descent on this ill-conditioned problem is slow, and the subspace is already correct. That gap is a budget statement, not a disagreement.) This is the key bridge to 10.3: make the map non-linear, add a non-linear activation, and the autoencoder stops being PCA and starts being something PCA cannot do. The Colab lab runs that version in PyTorch.

2. Concept Questions

  1. PCA optimizes total variance; t-SNE optimizes a KL divergence between neighbour-similarity distributions. On a linear Gaussian dataset, which should you use, and why does the “better” objective lose?
  2. Perplexity in t-SNE is a bandwidth. A colleague shows two t-SNE plots of the same data, one with visibly separated clusters and one with everything touching, and asks which is “really” true. What is the correct answer, and what would you write in the paper?
  3. Trustworthiness is 0.999 for a t-SNE embedding, yet almost no pairwise distance is preserved. Is that a contradiction? State precisely what each number certifies — and then say what a trustworthiness of 0.94 on pure noise implies about using this metric as evidence of structure.
  4. Why is a t-SNE embedding of a test set impossible with a fitted t-SNE object, and what does that imply about using these coordinates as features for a downstream classifier?
  5. The linear autoencoder provably converges to PCA. If a non-linear autoencoder with the same bottleneck is strictly more expressive, why is it not automatically the better representation?
Answers
  1. PCA wins, and the reason is that t-SNE’s objective is not an approximation of PCA’s on linear data. On a linear Gaussian, PCA is a sufficient statistic: the top-d subspace captures all the recoverable structure, and t-SNE has to spend its optimization finding that same subspace through a non-convex objective with a free bandwidth. Isomap recovers it too (it equals PCA’s subspace up to rotation on linear data), which is why neighbour_spread is the right way to compare. A method that is “more expressive” can still lose when the extra flexibility buys nothing.
  2. Neither is “really” true — separation in a t-SNE plot is a dial, not a finding. The perplexity parameter sets the bandwidth, and the cell below shows apparent cluster separation moving by 9× while the clustering itself is unchanged. What t-SNE legitimately reports is which points are neighbours of which, and that must be the claim. If you want a defensible global geometry, use MDS/Isomap and report distances with uncertainties; if you want a picture of local structure, use t-SNE/UMAP and say the axes are meaningless.
  3. No — they certify different things, which is exactly the trap. Trustworthiness asks whether each point’s embedding-neighbours were already neighbours in the original space: t-SNE scores 0.999, essentially perfect. The distance check asks whether the numerical distances survive, and they do not (0.2% do). t-SNE’s loss contains a term for the first and no constraint at all on the second. High trustworthiness plus destroyed distances is the normal output of t-SNE, not a defect. The follow-up is the important one: run trustworthiness on structureless Gaussian data and you still get 0.94, because in 5-D a random 2-D projection preserves “neighbourhoods” almost perfectly (in moderate dimensions all pairwise distances concentrate, so any point’s 10 nearest neighbours are roughly equidistant — the effect 9.2 flags as distance concentration). So a trustworthiness near 1 is a weak signal: only a large gap between your data’s value and the null value is evidence of anything.
  4. Because t-SNE has no out-of-sample transform. Its optimization is over the whole dataset jointly — the positions of every point depend on every other point, so there is no function \(f\) mapping a new \(x\) to coordinates. The same is true of most t-SNE pipelines in practice. So t-SNE coordinates cannot be features for a downstream model: the classifier would be trained on coordinates that will not exist at prediction time. (UMAP and most manifold methods do have a transform, which is the practical reason people prefer them for pipelines; the coordinates are still not distances.)
  5. Because a strictly larger function class is not a strictly better one. A non-linear autoencoder can represent the linear PCA solution exactly (initialize near zero, the non-linearities pass identity), so the minimum reconstruction error is ≤ PCA’s — but it is not attained at the PCA solution, because the network will happily use its extra capacity to fit the noise in directions that do not generalize. You are buying expressiveness with variance: the representation must be selected by held-out reconstruction, early stopping, or a bottleneck small enough to bite (10.5’s identical problem one layer up). The extra flexibility only pays when there is real non-linear structure to capture — the Swiss roll, and nothing else.

Drills

Exercises (with solutions)

Exercise 1. The picture is a dial, quantified. On the four-blob data, sweep perplexity ∈ {5, 10, 20, 40, 80, 160} and for each report (a) the mean inter-cluster centroid gap, (b) the ARI of K-means on the embedding against the true labels, and (c) trustworthiness(k=10). Report the ratio of largest to smallest gap across the sweep, and the same ratio for ARI and trustworthiness. Then: a colleague ships a figure with a chosen perplexity. Write the two sentences that must appear in the caption, and state the one quantity from your table that is perplexity-invariant and therefore safe to report.

Solution

The gap varies by about 20× across the sweep (perplexity 5 gives visibly huge separations; 160 collapses them) while ARI moves by a factor of 1.000 and trustworthiness by 1.006 — both flat. So the picture is a free parameter and the clustering is not. But note the trap in the last ratio: flat does not mean informative. The negative control in Exercise 2 shows trustworthiness is already 0.94 on pure noise, so a flat-0.99 trustworthiness is not by itself evidence of anything. The only quantity in this table that is genuinely safe to report is ARI against external labels — because it is the one that requires the ground truth you are actually trying to say something about. The caption sentences: (i) the axis scale and inter-cluster distances in this figure are not meaningful and must not be interpreted; (ii) the embedding is shown at perplexity = p and the recovered neighbourhood structure was stable across p ∈ [5, 160] — which is the honest form of a robustness claim, because the picture itself is not robust.

Exercise 2. Choosing the tool — and the negative control. Four datasets: (a) structureless 5-D Gaussian, (b) a 2-D plane embedded in 20 noisy dimensions, (c) a Swiss roll, (d) two concentric rings in 2-D. Each of (b)–(d) has a known generative coordinate (the first latent, the roll parameter \(t\), the radius). For every dataset × method in {PCA-2, Isomap-2, t-SNE} report coherence: the spread of that coordinate among each point’s 10 embedding-neighbours, in units of the coordinate’s own SD (lower = the embedding recovered the structure). Then run trustworthiness on the null dataset as a control, and write the routing rule — including the case where the intuitively-right method is the worst one.

Solution

Three results, and the third is the reason to run the control.

Linear truth (plane+noise): 0.613 / 0.651 / 0.624 — all within noise of each other, with PCA marginally ahead. No curved structure exists to be found, so the extra machinery of Isomap and t-SNE buys nothing and costs optimization. This is 9.2’s sufficiency result showing up as a measurement.

Curved truth (swiss roll): PCA 1.108 vs Isomap 0.770 and t-SNE 0.820. PCA is clearly worst, by 30–44%, and this is the case the page exists for. Isomap edges out t-SNE here, but read the next row before concluding which is better.

The surprise (concentric rings): PCA 0.796 beats both manifold methods — Isomap is the worst of the three at 1.002. Intuition says “rings are curved, so use the curved-structure method,” and that is exactly backwards. Rings already live in 2-D, so a linear projection keeps them intact and coherent; Isomap then does something actively unhelpful: it replaces the true distances with shortest-path distances on a k-NN graph, and a closed circular curve unrolls into a line, which re-scales the radius information the metric was supposed to preserve. The failure is in the one non-exact step of the algorithm, and it is invisible unless you check.

The control decides what any of this means. Trustworthiness on structureless Gaussian data is 0.776 (PCA), 0.755 (Isomap) and 0.936 (t-SNE) — all “high” — because in 5 dimensions all pairwise distances concentrate, so nearly any 2-D projection has plausible-looking local neighbourhoods. A trustworthiness near 1 is therefore weak evidence, and a t-SNE plot of pure noise will still look tidy. Routing rule, in order: run the negative control first and keep only statistics that separate the real data from it; if a linear projection already recovers the generative structure, stop there (PCA); reach for Isomap/MDS when the structure is genuinely curved and the data is higher-dimensional than its intrinsic dimension; use t-SNE/UMAP when what you need is a picture of local neighbourhoods and you will report it as such. And note the general lesson: no method dominates, and the two that are “supposed” to handle curvature are the ones that fail on a closed curve. Choose per data, verify per data.

Exercise 3. The autoencoder/PCA bridge, closed. (a) Prove that a tied, linear autoencoder minimizing \(\|Z - ZWW^\top\|_F^2\) has stationary points satisfying \(Z^\top Z W = W\Lambda\) for symmetric \(\Lambda\), and identify the solutions. (b) Verify numerically by gradient descent (as in Section 1) that the recovered subspace matches PCA’s. (c) Explain in one sentence why breaking the tie (a non-tied autoencoder, the usual architecture) does not change the linear result but does change the non-linear one. Then state what the Colab lab has to add to make the non-linear case strictly better than PCA.

Solution Differentiating \(L = \|Z - ZWW^\top\|_F^2\) gives \(\partial L/\partial W = 2Z^\top(ZWW^\top - Z)W\), which vanishes when \(Z^\top Z W = W W^\top W W^\top = W(W^\top W)\), i.e. \(Z^\top Z W = W\Lambda\) with \(\Lambda = W^\top W\) symmetric — the eigenvector equation for \(Z^\top Z\). Its solutions are \(W\)’s columns equal to the top eigenvectors, and by Eckart–Young (9.2) that is precisely the optimal low-rank reconstruction. (b) the Section-1 cell measures principal cosines of 0.997 and 0.990 between the two subspaces. (c) Untying removes the \(W^\top W\) symmetry so the stationary equations become \(Z^\top(Z - \hat Z)W = 0\) and \(\hat Z^\top(\hat Z - Z)W^\top = 0\) — for linear maps the decoupled solution is still the PCA subspace (the decoder is simply \(W^\top\) with a rescaling, which is absorbed by the scale degeneracy of a bilinear model), but with a non-linear encoder the invariance is gone and the encoder directions are no longer pinned to an eigendecomposition of \(Z^\top Z\). So the Colab lab must add exactly three things: a non-linear activation, a bottleneck small enough that the extra capacity cannot absorb the noise (else it overfits rather than compresses — 10.5), and held-out reconstruction or early stopping to pick the stopping point. The measurable claim to test there is the one from Section 1: does the non-linear encoder beat PCA on the Swiss roll, and by how much — while matching it on the Gaussian.

4. Advanced Information & Pitfalls

  • t-SNE has no out-of-sample transform. Positions are fitted jointly, so you cannot embed new points. This is a hard architectural limit, not a missing feature, and it rules out using t-SNE coordinates as model features.
  • Perplexity is a resolution control, not a tuning target — the sweep above moves apparent cluster separation 9× at fixed clustering. Tune it against a question (“how large a neighbourhood do I claim?”) and report the neighbourhood claim, never the gap.
  • Axes, distances and areas in a t-SNE plot are meaningless. Only the neighbour graph is meaningful. (If you need a map, use MDS/Isomap, whose axes are the reconstructed coordinates.)
  • Cluster sizes in t-SNE plots are not comparable across clusters or across runs. Density in the embedding is shaped by the density in the data plus the bandwidth, so a dense region gets spread out and a sparse one contracts.
  • UMAP vs t-SNE: same motivation, different objective (fuzzy-set cross-entropy vs KL against a Student-t). UMAP is usually faster and its layouts tend to be more globally coherent, and — unlike t-SNE — UMAP objects have .transform(). Neither is a distance-preserving map.
  • Isomap/MDS need the full pairwise distance matrix — O(n²) memory. On high-dimensional data, k-NN-graph approximations (LLE, UMAP) or metric="precomputed" chunking are the practical route.
  • Non-linear ≠ better. Every method here spends expressiveness; on linear data PCA wins and t-SNE is strictly worse. Establish linear structure first (9.2’s scree/elbow) before reaching for these.
  • A manifold is an assumption, not a finding. That the data concentrates near a low-dimensional surface is a claim with evidence (9.1’s standard: stability, interpretability, usefulness). Do not fit a manifold to noise and read structure into the output.

Read & Refactor AI Code

This AI-generated “clustering on t-SNE features” has three bugs. Run, diagnose, fix.

What’s wrong, and the fix
  1. Apparent separation is a knob, not a measurement. This number is a deterministic function of perplexity, which the next line then optimizes — so the pipeline is explicitly selecting the parameter that makes its own reported statistic largest. The cell in Section 3 shows this quantity moving 9× while the actual clustering (ARI vs true labels) never changes. The fix: if you want a separation statistic, compute it in a geometry that has one (MDS/Isomap distances, or a silhouette in the original space), and never let a reporting metric choose a hyperparameter.
  2. Choosing perplexity by eye is choosing a picture. “Cleanest-looking” is a criterion on the rendering, and it is the criterion most vulnerable to the failure mode this page is about. Tune perplexity against a substantive target instead (a held-out neighbourhood consistency, or a downstream 9.6-style stability criterion), and report a sweep.
  3. t-SNE coordinates as a feature matrix is not a pipeline. A KMeans fit on Z cannot score a new sample: t-SNE has no .transform, so at prediction time there is no way to place new data in the same coordinates — the model is unreproducible outside the training set. Worse, the distances in Z are not meaningful (0.2% of pairwise distances survive), so even in-sample the clustering is built on a distorted geometry. This is why pipelines use UMAP (which does have .transform) or the raw features, and why 9.6 insists on a pre-registered policy evaluated by resampling stability rather than by how the picture reads.

5. Advanced Questions

Q1. Why does t-SNE’s objective make the embedding’s scale undetermined, and what is the practical consequence for reading two t-SNE plots side by side? Work from the form of the KL objective and show the scale symmetry explicitly. Then explain why adding a repulsive term (as UMAP does) partially fixes this and why the fix is only partial.

Q2. Derive the geodesic-distance construction behind Isomap and show that on a linear dataset it reduces (up to rotation) to PCA. Then construct a dataset where Isomap and PCA agree and one where Isomap fails, and explain the failure in terms of the k-NN graph step (the only non-exact part of the algorithm).

Hints Q1: the Student-t target \(Q_{ij} \propto (1 + \|y_i - y_j\|^2)^{-1}\) plus the \(\sum_j Q_{ij} = 1\) constraint; try scaling all \(y\) by a constant and see what KL does. Q2: Isomap = shortest paths on a k-NN graph, then classical MDS on that distance matrix. Ask when the graph is disconnected, and what MDS does with an indefinite distance matrix.

6. Complex Questions

C1 (embeddings × 4.1 generalization × 9.6 practical). Embeddings are fitted on data, so their “error” is a resampling question like any other estimator. Design a protocol that measures the stability of a t-SNE/UMAP embedding under bootstrap resampling (e.g. overlap of the k-NN graph across resamples, Procrustes-aligned), apply it to real structure vs null Gaussian data, and report the statistic that separates the two. Then state the pre-registered rule: below what stability should you refuse to report the embedding at all?

C2 (embeddings × 10.3 multilayer networks × 5.6 high-dimensional). The autoencoder as a supervised-target alternative: fit a non-linear autoencoder with a 2-unit bottleneck on high-dimensional data (p ≫ n), then use the learned 2-D code as features for a classifier, and compare against (a) PCA-2 features, (b) a supervised 2-component model (5.5’s PLS), and (c) raw features. Report held-out performance for each. When does the unsupervised bottleneck win, and what is the diagnostic that predicts it in advance? Connect to the 9.2 lesson that variance order ≠ relevance order.

7. Summary

  • PCA finds the best linear shadow; on curved data no number of components will unroll a Swiss roll. Isomap/MDS buy a defensible geometry by preserving (geodesic) distances.
  • t-SNE optimizes a KL divergence over neighbour similarities, not distances. On the 4-blob data it keeps 100% of neighbourhoods and 0.2% of distance magnitudes — high trustworthiness and destroyed distances are its normal output, not a defect.
  • Perplexity is a picture dial: apparent cluster separation moves 9× across a sweep while ARI stays at 1.000. Never report the gap, never tune perplexity by eye, and never read t-SNE axes, distances or areas.
  • t-SNE has no out-of-sample transform, so its coordinates cannot be model features or scored on new data. UMAP is the pipeline-friendly cousin (has .transform), and is Colab-only here.
  • A tied, linear autoencoder provably is PCA (stationary points satisfy \(Z^\top Z W = W\Lambda\)); verified at principal cosines 0.997/0.990. Non-linearity only pays when there is real non-linear structure — and costs variance, so the bottleneck needs held-out selection.
  • Every method on this page spends expressiveness, and on linear data PCA beats all of them. Establish linear structure first, then reach for curvature.
  • Embeddings are fitted quantities: their stability under resampling is evidence, and their pictures are claims. A manifold is an assumption; run the negative control (9.6’s refusal rule) before believing one.
