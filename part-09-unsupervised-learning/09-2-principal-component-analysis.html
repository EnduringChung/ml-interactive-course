9.2 Principal Component Analysis

PCA finds the orthogonal directions along which the data varies most, and re-expresses it in those directions. It is simultaneously a dimension-reduction tool, a decorrelation machine, a visualization device, and the algebraic backbone of ridge (5.2) and PCR (5.5) — the most reused object in applied statistics.

Tier 1 — every cell below runs live.

1. Basic Concept

PC1 = the unit vector w maximizing Var(Xw) = wᵀΣw subject to ‖w‖ = 1 — the solution is the top eigenvector of the covariance matrix; subsequent PCs are the next eigenvectors, orthogonal to the rest. Equivalent view: PCA is the SVD of the (centered) data matrix.

2. Concept Questions

  1. PCA is unsupervised — state precisely what it does not look at, and recall the page where that blindness cost it (5.5).
  2. Why must features be centered before PCA? Scaled? When is scaling the wrong choice?
  3. Your PC1 has loadings (−0.7, +0.7, 0.05, 0.1). Interpret it — and state the two sign/scale caveats.
  4. What does “PCA loses information” mean numerically, and which quantity measures exactly what’s lost per component?
  5. PCA on raw counts (skewed, huge-variance features) found “PC1 = one gene.” Diagnose, and name the two standard fixes.
Answers
  1. It never sees y. Consequence: PCs rank by X-variance, which may be irrelevant to the response — the low-variance-signal pathology (5.5 Exercise 2), and the supervised alternative (PLS) exists for exactly this.
  2. Center: PCA finds directions through the origin of the data cloud — uncentered, PC1 points at the mean (garbage directions). Scale: PCA’s criterion is variance — without scaling, big-unit features own the components; scale whenever features have arbitrary units. Wrong to scale: when features share a natural unit and their variances ARE the signal (e.g., same-assay measurements) — then raw-covariance PCA respects the physics.
  3. “A contrast: feature 1 loads negative, feature 2 positive, equally; features 3–4 uninvolved” — PC1 is roughly feature2 − feature1. Caveats: (a) sign is arbitrary (−w is the same component — sklearn flips signs by its own convention); (b) loadings are unit-scaled — interpret the pattern (ratios, signs), not absolute numbers.
  4. Reconstruction error: ‖X − X_M‖² = Σ_{j>M} λⱼ (sum of discarded eigenvalues) — PCA drops exactly the orthogonal variance along dropped directions, and explained_variance_ratio_ is the kept fraction. Loss is orthogonal-projection loss: the best rank-M summary possible (Eckart–Young: PCA is optimal in Frobenius norm).
  5. Diagnosis: unscaled, skewed data — the huge-variance gene dominates PC1 by units, not biology. Fixes: log-transform skewed features (0.4), then standardize. Then PC1 becomes a pattern, not a unit artifact.

3. Drills & Exercises

Drills

Predict, then run.

Exercises (with solutions)

Exercise 1. PCA by hand, via covariance eigendecomposition and via SVD: center a dataset, compute Σ = XᶜᵀXᶜ/(n−1), take np.linalg.eigh, and compare its eigenpairs to sklearn’s PCA output (match signs, compare explained ratios). Then do it via np.linalg.svd and verify the identity λⱼ = sⱼ²/(n−1). Owning this equivalence demystifies every PCA discussion in the course.

Solution
Three roads, one decomposition. Eigen-route exposes “eigenvectors of covariance”; SVD-route exposes “best rank-M projection” (Eckart–Young); sklearn emits the latter. Any PCA claim you’ll ever read reduces to objects you just computed.

Exercise 2. The scree decision, done with eyes: on a dataset with a planted structure (3 latent factors + noise, p = 12), plot (a) explained-variance ratios, (b) cumulative variance, (c) the reconstruction error as M grows. Mark where the scree “elbows” and where reconstruction error flattens. Then verify against the truth: M = 3 captures the planted structure. Finally, state when the elbow is ambiguous and which tool arbitrates (6.5’s dial logic, transplanted).

Solution
Planted rank shows as the scree’s cliff (huge λ₁₋₃, noise-floor λ₄₊). Ambiguous elbows (gradual decay) happen when signal and noise variances blend — then the choice is a bias–variance trade like any M-selection (5.5 CV-tunes M; 9.6 stability-checks it), and visualization is a legitimate “M = 2 by fiat” use.

Exercise 3. Biplot discipline: on the 4-feature cohort-style data below, produce (a) PC1/PC2 score scatter, (b) loading vectors overlaid, (c) each feature’s correlation with PC1/PC2 as a table. Interpret PC1 in a sentence a domain reader could accept, and identify which feature the biplot says is redundant (nearly collinear with another) — verify by direct correlation. Note explicitly what a biplot cannot tell you (name the supervised question it ignores).

Solution
The biplot’s geometry: features pointing the same direction are correlated (expr1/expr2 nearly overlap — redundancy visible before any correlation call); features opposing are anti-correlated; scores near a loading tip have high values of that feature. What it can’t say: anything about y — PCA is blind to outcomes by construction (5.5), and “PC1 = severity” needs external validation (9.1’s reification trap).

4. Advanced Information & Pitfalls

  • PCA on the covariance vs correlation matrix: scaling = correlation-PCA; raw = covariance-PCA — a modeling choice with the 9.1 assumption weight (Exercise units; Q2b).
  • PCA is not feature selection: components are combinations of all features — sparse variants (sparse PCA) exist if you need actual feature drops (5.1’s selection, rotated).
  • Outliers own variance: one extreme point can be PC1; robust PCA (or inspection before fitting) for dirty data (2.10 leverage, unsupervised edition).
  • PCA inside pipelines: fit on training folds only — the leakage law (0.5); Pipeline(StandardScaler(), PCA(), model) is the mechanical guarantee (5.5 Exercise 3).
  • Interpretation limits: loadings are basis vectors of a rotated coordinate system — components can mix causally unrelated features; correlations between scores and external variables are the validation, not the discovery (9.1 Q3).
  • Sign and rotation freedom: eigenvectors are sign-arbitrary; with near-equal eigenvalues, subspaces rotate freely — small data perturbations can “swap” PCs (stability diagnostics again, 9.6).

What’s wrong, and the fix

  2. Choosing the component by correlation with y: (a) that’s supervised selection wearing PCA clothes — the honest tool is PLS/PCR-with-CV (5.5); (b) with 2 components the “choice” is trivial anyway. Keep PCA unsupervised and use all M components downstream, or justify M by variance/CV.
  3. Two different PCAs for train/test: bases must match — fit the transformer on training data, transform test (the Pipeline law, 0.5).

5. Advanced Questions

Q1. Prove PCA’s optimality (Eckart–Young): among all rank-M approximations C of centered X, PCA’s reconstruction X·V_M·V_Mᵀ (V_M = top-M right singular vectors) minimizes ‖X − C‖_F. Verify numerically that no other rank-2 orthogonal projection of a fixed dataset achieves lower reconstruction error, and connect the result to the SVD identity λⱼ = sⱼ²/(n−1) from Exercise 1.

Hint Eckart–Young: truncating the SVD at rank M minimizes Frobenius error; PCA’s subspace IS the top-M right singular subspace.

Solution</summary»

By the SVD X = USVᵀ, the best rank-M approximation in Frobenius norm is U_M S_M V_Mᵀ (Eckart–Young theorem) — keeping the M largest singular values. PCA’s projection X V_M V_Mᵀ is exactly this object; the discarded error is Σ_{j>M} sⱼ² = (n−1)Σ_{j>M} λⱼ.

PCA and truncated SVD coincide to float precision; any other rank-2 projection loses strictly more — PCA is the unique best low-rank linear summary (up to rotation within the subspace). This optimality is in X’s own geometry — nothing about y (5.5’s recurring caveat).

Q2. PCA stability under correlated noise: generate data with a planted PC1 and increasing noise correlation among the noise features; measure (a) the angle between refit PC1 across bootstrap resamples and (b) the explained-variance-ratio stability, as noise correlation grows. Show PC subspace stability degrading before the variance ratio does — then write the reporting rule: which PCA claims need stability analysis (subspace interpretation) and which are robust (aggregate variance summaries)?

Solution
As noise features correlate, the noise block gains a single dominant direction (eigenvalues 0.2×6 and 5.8 at ρ = 0.8, versus seven ties at ρ = 0), and that — not the planted signal — is what stabilizes PC1. The angles fall (72.6° → 7.0° → 2.2°) as ρ rises, and the explained-variance summary moves only slightly (PC1’s SD across resamples 0.009 → 0.025 → 0.015). So the intuition to discard is “correlated noise destabilizes the top eigenvector.” The truth is the reverse, and the reason is instructive: after StandardScaler every column has variance 1, so the planted column (raw variance 9) has been demoted to a peer of the seven noise columns. With ρ = 0 those seven are exchangeable, PC1 is free to be any mixture of them, and its identity is essentially arbitrary — which is why it wobbles by 72°. At ρ = 0.8 they share one common direction worth ~74% of the block’s variance, so PC1 locks onto it. Identifiability here comes from the noise block’s structure, not from the signal’s. Two reporting rules follow. Subspace stability, not variance explained, is what licenses the phrase “PC1 captures X% of the variance” — quote the EVR only alongside a bootstrap angle, because an EVR of 0.74 here describes a direction that has nothing to do with the planted latent factor. And note the failure mode this experiment nearly walked into: the original covariance was written 1 + rho * (ones − eye), which adds rho to the off-diagonals too, implying correlation 1+rho > 1. That matrix is indefinite (eigenvalues −0.4×6), and multivariate_normal does not raise — it projects onto the nearest PSD matrix, so the “noise” quietly became seven near-identical columns and the experiment ran to completion producing a clean, confident, meaningless answer.

6. Complex Questions

C1 (PCA × 7.4 forests × 3.6 GLM). Representation learning bake-off: on wide data (n = 150, p = 100, mixed linear/nonlinear signals), compare downstream classifiers trained on (a) raw features, (b) PCA-20 scores, (c) PCA-5 scores — nested CV. When does compression help (noise dilution) and when hurt (nonlinear signal spread across PCs)? Connect to 5.5’s PCR findings and write the dimension-reduction routing rule: raw vs compressed, by signal type and n/p ratio.

C2 (PCA × 9.4 K-means × 12.x batch stub). The cluster-on-PCs pipeline: cluster (K-means, k = 3) on (a) raw scaled features and (b) the top-3 PC scores of wide data with planted clusters and a planted batch direction. Measure agreement with true biological clusters vs with batches, both pipelines. Does PCA-to-3 dims remove the batch artifact or preserve it (hint: batch is a variance direction)? Write the caution about “we clustered in PC space, so it’s denoised” — and the correct order of operations (batch audit before, not after, compression).

7. Summary

  • PCA = eigendecomposition of covariance = SVD of centered data: directions of maximal variance, orthogonally.
  • Three objects: scores (data in PC space), loadings (the rotation), explained-variance ratios (the budget).
  • Center always; scale unless units are meaningful; fit inside pipelines; the outcome-blindness is structural.
  • Reconstruction loss = dropped eigenvalues; PCA is the optimal rank-M summary (Eckart–Young) in X’s geometry.
  • Scree picks M when the cliff is real; CV picks it when it’s a bias–variance trade.
  • Interpretation claims need subspace stability; variance claims are sturdier.
  • PCA compresses; the next problem is what to do when the data is incomplete — 9.3 Missing values and matrix completion.
