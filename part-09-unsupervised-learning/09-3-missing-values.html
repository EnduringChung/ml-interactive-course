9.3 Missing Values & Matrix Completion

Real data has holes: dropped measurements, failed assays, unanswered questions. What you do with them is a modeling decision — delete cases, fill with means, model the missingness, or exploit low-rank structure — and each choice encodes an assumption about why the values are missing. This page builds the taxonomy and the toolkit.

Tier 1 — every cell below runs live.

1. Basic Concept

The missingness taxonomy (Rubin):

  • MCAR — missing completely at random: the hole is independent of everything (a scanner glitch).
  • MAR — missing at random: the hole depends on observed variables only (young patients skip a costly scan).
  • MNAR — missing depends on the missing value itself (the sickest patients’ values are unmeasurable).

The mechanism determines the remedy: deletion is unbiased only under MCAR; imputation models must capture MAR dependencies; MNAR needs explicit missingness modeling (or honesty about limits).

2. Concept Questions

  1. Complete-case (listwise) deletion: under which mechanism is it unbiased, what does it cost even then, and when is it catastrophic?
  2. Mean imputation’s two distinct sins — one statistical (variance), one geometric (for distances/clustering)?
  3. KNN imputation: what structure does it exploit, and what happens to it as dimension grows (2.9)?
  4. Why is “impute, then treat imputed values as real” in downstream inference dishonest — and what’s the standard fix’s name?
  5. A recommender-style matrix (users × items) is 98% missing. Which paradigm applies, and why is this not an imputation problem in the usual sense?
Answers
  1. Unbiased under MCAR only. Cost even then: smaller n and wider SEs — you paid data for holes. Catastrophic under MAR/MNAR with systematic missingness (e.g., all severe cases missing one assay): the “complete” sample is a biased population (1.1’s summary trap, dataset edition).
  2. Statistical: imputed values are the mean — zero deviation — so variances/correlations shrink toward 0 (n identical contributions). Geometric: every imputed row sits exactly on the mean-point, corrupting distances and cluster structure (9.1’s geometry law).
  3. It exploits local correlation structure: a missing value is filled from similar rows’ values — works while neighbors are informative; under high dimension, neighborhoods blow up and “nearest” becomes meaningless (curse of dimensionality, 2.9).
  4. Imputed entries are guesses with model-derived uncertainty; treating them as observations understates every SE and overstates every p-value — the imputation model’s uncertainty must propagate. The classical fix: multiple imputation (Rubin) — draw several imputations, analyze each, pool with Rubin’s rules. (Modern shortcut: models that treat missingness natively, 7.6.)
  5. Low-rank matrix completion (Netflix problem): the matrix’s rank structure (users × items have few latent factors) makes recovery possible from sparse entries — the goal is predicting held-out entries, not filling a covariate matrix for a model. Different objective, different machinery (SVD-based, Exercise 3).

3. Drills & Exercises

Drills

Predict, then run.

Exercises (with solutions)

Exercise 1. Deletion vs imputation under three mechanisms: build y = 2x + ε and censor x (30%) under MCAR, MAR (missing when another observed variable z > 0), and MNAR (missing when |x| large). For each: fit OLS on complete cases and on mean-imputed data; compare slope estimates to truth. Which mechanism breaks deletion? Which breaks naive imputation worse? Write the decision rule.

Solution
Reading: deletion is fine under MCAR, biased under MAR (dropping by z distorts x’s range) and MNAR; naive mean-imputation is acceptable-ish under MCAR, but under MNAR it fabricates average-x values for exactly the extreme cases — often the worse distortion. Decision rule: diagnose the mechanism first (is missingness related to observed data? to itself?); delete freely only under MCAR; under MAR use imputation that models the observed-data dependencies; under MNAR, no mechanical fix — model the missingness explicitly or report the limitation.

Exercise 2. KNNImputer vs IterativeImputer on structured data: build a 4-feature matrix with two correlated pairs (x₂ ≈ x₁ + noise; x₄ ≈ −x₃ + noise) and 20% MCAR holes. Compare fill quality (MAE on held-out true values) for mean, KNN (k ∈ {2, 5, 15}), and IterativeImputer (round-robin regression). Which wins, and what data property does its win exploit?

Solution
Iterative (and small-k KNN) win by exploiting the pairwise correlation structure — each imputation is a mini-regression on the other features, exactly what the correlated pairs make learnable. Large k blurs toward the mean; the mean ignores structure entirely. The property exploited: conditional correlation among observed features — the more structure your matrix has, the more sophisticated imputation pays.

Exercise 3. Low-rank matrix completion by alternating SVD: build a ratings-like matrix with true rank 3 (users × items via 3 latent factors), mask 80% at random. Implement iterative SVD completion: initialize missing entries with the matrix mean; repeat {SVD at rank 3, replace missing entries with reconstruction} until convergence. Report recovery error on held-out entries vs the mean-fill baseline. Then verify the impossibility intuition: rerun on a full-rank (random) matrix — recovery collapses.

Solution
The alternation works because a low-rank matrix is determined by far fewer entries than it contains — the SVD projection strips noise from the guessed entries and the observed entries anchor the truth, iterating toward consistency. Random matrices have no such compression: completion is impossible, and the algorithm honestly fails. This is the Netflix-problem paradigm: recovery from structure, not imputation from covariates.

4. Advanced Information & Pitfalls

  • Diagnose before treating: build missingness indicators and test them against everything observed (chi-square/logistic) — discovered structure means MAR and exploitable signal; undiagnosed MNAR silently biases everything downstream (1.4’s assumption discipline).
  • The missingness indicator trick (drill 4): adding “was-missing” as a feature lets tree/boosting models use the pattern — legitimate under MAR-ish mechanisms, and standard practice in Kaggle-grade pipelines; but it assumes the pattern is informative, which MNAR can violate subtly.
  • Impute inside pipelines: imputers are fitted statistics (means, neighbor structure, regression coefficients) — fit per fold, transform test (0.5 law); fitting the imputer on all data leaks.
  • Dropping columns with high missingness is also a modeling decision: threshold-based dropping (“>40% missing → drop”) is arbitrary unless tied to mechanism reasoning; a column that’s missing because of the outcome is informative, not broken.
  • sklearn’s IterativeImputer is experimental (enable_iterative_imputer import) — it’s MICE-flavored; still single-imputation: uncertainty propagation needs multiple draws (or a native-NaN model like 7.6’s boosters).
  • Matrix completion ≠ imputation: completion predicts held-out entries of one big low-rank matrix (no response variable); imputation fills covariates for downstream models. Choose by what the filled values are for.

What’s wrong, and the fix
  1. Imputer fit before split: means/structure from test rows entered training — leakage (0.5). Imputer inside the pipeline.
  2. Mean-impute-then-cluster: imputed rows sit at the mean point, corrupting distances and fabricating a “central” mega-cluster (Concept 2b); use KNN-imputation, native-NaN models, or missingness-aware clustering.
  3. Dropped informative column: missingness correlated with y is signal — dropping it discards mechanism information; keep it with an indicator, or model the mechanism (drill 4).

5. Advanced Questions

Q1. Prove mean-imputation’s variance shrinkage: for x with variance σ² and fraction p missing-at-random imputed with x̄, the imputed vector’s variance is (1−p)σ² (+ a p(1−p)x̄-level correction of order 1/n). Verify by simulation across p ∈ {0, .25, .5, .75}, and derive the same shrinkage for correlations between two independently-imputed columns. What does this imply for downstream PCA/clustering on naively imputed matrices?

Hint Imputed entries contribute zero deviation from the mean by construction; compute Var = (1/n)Σ(observed deviations² + p·0²).
Solution

Imputed entries equal x̄ exactly, so Σ(xᵢ − x̄)² accumulates only over the (1−p)n observed rows: Var ≈ (1−p)σ² (exactly, for MCAR, in expectation). For two columns independently imputed, imputed positions are mean-valued in both — cross-products with imputed rows contribute 0 in deviation — correlations shrink toward 0 similarly. Simulation:

Downstream consequence: naively mean-imputed matrices show shrunken variances and correlations — PCA underestimates structure, clusters blur toward the centroid, loadings flatten (9.2). Every structure-seeking method sees a demorphosed world; the fix is uncertainty-aware imputation or methods treating missingness natively.

Q2. Missingness-forest vs MICE-style imputation on MAR data: simulate MAR missingness (probability of missing x₁ increases with observed x₂) with x₁ genuinely needed downstream; compare (a) KNN/Iterative imputation then logistic, (b) HistGradientBoosting with native NaN (7.6), (c) mean-impute + missingness-indicator logistic. Report downstream test accuracy/AUC. Which approach captures the MAR information, and what does the native-NaN win tell you about where imputation models actually earn their keep?

Solution
All imputation-and-indicator approaches capture most of the MAR information (the pattern is observable in x₂ and indicators); native-NaN boosting matches without maintaining an imputation model — its per-split missing-branch learns the pattern where it matters for the decision. The broader lesson: imputation models earn their keep by preserving the joint structure (correlations, mechanism) that the downstream task uses — when the downstream learner can ingest missingness natively and the pattern is simple, the elaborate imputer is optional; when inference (not prediction) is the goal, MICE-style multiple imputation remains the principled standard.

7. Summary

  • Missingness has mechanisms (MCAR/MAR/MNAR); the mechanism, not convenience, picks the remedy.
  • Deletion: unbiased only under MCAR, always costly; catastrophic under systematic missingness.
  • Mean imputation: shrinks variance and correlations, wrecks geometry — a placeholder, not a method.
  • KNN/Iterative imputers exploit observed-feature structure; fit them inside CV folds; uncertainty needs multiple imputation or native-NaN models.
  • Missingness indicators turn holes into features — legitimate when the pattern is informative; an assumption when it isn’t.
  • Low-rank completion solves the sparse-matrix problem (Netflix) from rank structure — impossible without it.
  • Every choice here is a missingness-mechanism assumption — diagnose the holes before modeling them.
