5.2 Ridge Regression

Ridge regression keeps every feature but shrinks its coefficient toward zero, buying variance down with bias up. It is the canonical answer to collinearity (2.5) and the first stopping point on the regularization dial that Part 5 spends three pages exploring.

Tier 1 — every cell below runs live.

1. Basic Concept

Ridge replaces least squares with penalized least squares:

\[\hat{\beta}^{\text{ridge}} = \arg\min_\beta \Big\{ \sum_i (y_i - \beta_0 - x_i^\top\beta)^2 + \lambda \sum_j \beta_j^2 \Big\}\]

Excluding the intercept from the penalty (as above — always, in practice): the center of the data shouldn’t be regularized. The solution has a closed form:

\[\hat{\beta} = (X^\top X + \lambda I)^{-1} X^\top y\]

λ > 0 makes the matrix invertible even when \(X^\top X\) is not — ridge exists precisely where OLS breaks (2.5’s singular design). The closed form, verified against sklearn (on centered data, so no intercept needed):

2. Concept Questions

  1. Why is ridge not scale-equivariant, and what preprocessing does that force on you?
  2. λ = 0 recovers which estimator? λ → ∞ recovers which prediction rule?
  3. Show from \((X^\top X + \lambda I)^{-1}\) why ridge is computable when p > n while OLS is not.
  4. Your colleague ridge-fits and then reports “effect of xⱼ = β̂ⱼ, p < 0.05.” Two problems with that sentence?
  5. In the bias–variance decomposition at fixed x₀, what does increasing λ do to each term — and why does total error eventually rise?
Answers
  1. The penalty Σβⱼ² depends on coefficient magnitudes, so multiplying xⱼ by 10 (dividing βⱼ by 10) changes the penalty for the same fit. Standardize features first — the penalty then says “each SD of signal costs equally.”
  2. λ = 0 → OLS (exact). λ → ∞ → coefficients → 0 → predictions collapse to ȳ (the mean-model of 2.1) — ridge interpolates between OLS and the null model.
  3. \((X^\top X + \lambda I)\) adds λ to every eigenvalue; even if \(X^\top X\) has zero eigenvalues (p ≥ n or perfect collinearity), all become ≥ λ > 0 — invertible. OLS needs \(X^\top X\) itself invertible.
    1. β̂ⱼ is shrunk — it underestimates the effect by design; quoting it as an effect size biases every claim toward zero. (2) The usual SEs/p-values don’t apply to penalized fits (4.6’s warning) — ridge is for prediction; for inference use OLS on selected/refit models or Bayesian machinery.
  5. Variance falls (shrunk coefficients wobble less across training sets), bias² rises (systematically short of the truth), σ² untouched. Total = U-shaped in λ: the sweet spot is where marginal variance savings stop exceeding marginal bias cost.

Drills

Exercises (with solutions)

Exercise 1. Prove the closed form: expand \(\sum(y - X\beta)^2 + \lambda\beta^\top\beta\), differentiate in β, set to zero, solve. Then verify numerically that the ridge solution equals OLS on the augmented dataset obtained by appending rows of zeros y=0 (a trick: penalized = unpenalized with pseudo-data). What does each pseudo-row “say” to the fit?

Solution

Objective: \((y - X\beta)^\top(y - X\beta) + \lambda\beta^\top\beta\). Gradient: \(-2X^\top(y - X\beta) + 2\lambda\beta = 0 \Rightarrow (X^\top X + \lambda I)\beta = X^\top y \Rightarrow \hat\beta = (X^\top X + \lambda I)^{-1}X^\top y\). The augmentation: appending √λ·I as rows with targets 0 makes the unpenalized RSS on the augmented data exactly RSS + λ‖β‖² — so OLS-on-augmented = ridge.

Each pseudo-row whispers: “imagine an observation where all features are √λ and the outcome is 0 — a fit with large coefficients would miss it.” That’s the prior in data clothing (and the Bayesian MAP connection of Complex C2).

Exercise 2. The collinearity payoff, priced: build y = 2x₁ + ε with x₂ = x₁ + noise (heavy collinearity, n = 60). For λ ∈ {0, 0.1, 1, 10, 100}: (a) record the variance of β̂₁ across 500 training sets (fix the scale by standardizing), (b) record test MSE on fresh data. Produce the two-row table. Which λ minimizes variance, which minimizes test MSE, and how does the OLS row (λ = 0) illustrate 2.5’s “coefficients see-saw”?

Solution
SD of β̂₁ collapses monotonically as λ grows (variance cure), while test MSE bottoms around λ ≈ 1–10 and rises at λ = 100 (bias takes over) — the U-curve in one table. At λ = 0, note how unstable the standardized coefficient is despite the model predicting fine: collinearity wrecks interpretation, not prediction — and ridge is the interpretation-preserving compromise.

Exercise 3. Tune λ honestly (4.6): with RidgeCV and with GridSearchCV-in-pipeline, select α on training data over a log grid; report the chosen α, its CV score, and a nested outer estimate of the whole procedure. Then compare the nested estimate against the naive best_score_ — quantify the selection optimism you now expect.

Solution
best_score_ sits above the nested estimate by the usual order-statistic margin — now predictable, because you’ve measured this gap three ways (4.1 Q2, 4.3 Exercise 2, here).

4. Advanced Information & Pitfalls

  • Standardize first — no exceptions. Unscaled ridge penalizes measurement units, not information; the pipeline is the enforcement mechanism (0.5).
  • Ridge coefficients are biased by construction — never quote them as effect sizes or feed them to inference machinery. If you need effects after shrinkage: post-ridge OLS refit on the (all) features with honest caveats, or Bayesian ridge with credible intervals.
  • The intercept is unpenalized (sklearn’s fit_intercept=True default) — centering by the intercept handles the mean; penalizing it would fight the data’s level for no reason.
  • λ’s meaning is n-dependent: sklearn minimizes \(\frac{1}{2n}\|y-X\beta\|^2 + \alpha\|\beta\|^2\)-style objectives, so “α = 1” means different shrinkage at n = 100 vs n = 100,000. Tune per dataset, never copy alphas between studies.
  • Ridge keeps all p features — if p ≫ n and most features are dead, ridge’s predictions can still be good but its weight spread is ugly; that’s lasso territory (5.3).
  • The SVD view: ridge divides each principal-component direction’s contribution by (1 + λ/sᵢ²) — low-variance directions (9.2 preview) are shrunk hardest. Ridge is soft PCR, which is exactly 5.5’s comparison.
  • Correlated groups share credit smoothly — two near-duplicates get similar shrunken coefficients (no coin-flip), which is often the scientifically honest answer, and exactly where lasso behaves worse (5.4).

5. Advanced Questions

Q1. Using the SVD \(X = U S V^\top\), derive the ridge fit as \(\hat y = U\, \text{diag}\!\left(\frac{s_i^2}{s_i^2 + \lambda}\right) U^\top y\) and conclude: (a) ridge shrinks each principal direction by the factor \(s_i^2/(s_i^2 + \lambda)\); (b) directions with tiny \(s_i\) (near-collinearity) are crushed first. Verify numerically on a rank-deficient design that ridge’s fitted values are finite and stable while OLS’s minimum-norm solution swings wildly across resamples.

Hint OLS fitted values are \(U U^\top y\) (projection); ridge replaces the indicator \(1\{s_i > 0\}\)-style weights with smooth shrink factors.
Solution

\(\hat\beta = V\,\text{diag}\!\left(\frac{s_i}{s_i^2+\lambda}\right)U^\top y\), so \(\hat y = X\hat\beta = U\,\text{diag}\!\left(\frac{s_i^2}{s_i^2+\lambda}\right)U^\top y\): each SVD direction passes through with a shrink factor — small-\(s_i\) directions (where OLS divides by \(s_i\), amplifying noise) are suppressed. Verification:

The near-collinear second column makes OLS’s fitted surface (here, its 1-var analogue’s slope estimate) jitter; ridge’s shrink factor on the small-\(s\) direction stabilizes it. This SVD picture is also the precise link to PCR (5.5): PCR truncates small directions (hard), ridge damps them (soft).

Q2. Bayesian ridge: with a Gaussian prior \(\beta \sim N(0, \tau^2 I)\) and Gaussian likelihood, show the MAP estimate equals ridge with \(\lambda = \sigma^2/\tau^2\). Verify numerically that Ridge(alpha)’s solution matches the posterior mode from a simple Gaussian-prior least-squares objective. What does this buy you (credible intervals, prior knowledge encoding) and what does it cost (name the assumption)?

Hint Maximize the log posterior: log-likelihood − ‖β‖²/(2τ²); match coefficients with the ridge objective.
Solution

Log posterior ∝ \(-\frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\|y - X\beta\|^2 - \frac{1}{2\tau^2}\|\beta\|^2\). Multiplying by \(2\sigma^2\): \(\|y-X\beta\|^2 + \frac{\sigma^2}{\tau^2}\|\beta\|^2\) — ridge with \(\lambda = \sigma^2/\tau^2\). The MAP of “Gaussian likelihood + zero-mean Gaussian prior” IS ridge.

Buy: a principled reading of λ (prior variance ratio), credible intervals from the posterior (Gaussian: covariance \((X^\top X/\sigma^2 + I/\tau^2)^{-1}\)), and a place to encode genuine prior knowledge (e.g., effects near zero and bounded). Cost: the prior assumption itself — credible intervals are statements conditional on Gaussianity of effects, and treating the prior as “just regularization” while quoting Bayesian intervals is having it both ways.

6. Complex Questions

C1 (ridge × 4.4 bias–variance). Measure the full decomposition across λ: 300 training sets (n = 40, p = 20, 5 real features), record bias²/variance of predictions on a fixed grid for λ ∈ {0.01, 1, 100}. Show the variance curve falling monotonically, bias² rising, and the sum bottoming between — then locate the λ that CV chose on one representative dataset and mark it on the decomposition. How close is CV’s choice to the decomposition optimum?

C2 (ridge × 9.2 PCA stub × 5.5 preview). Construct the classic pathological direction: y driven by a component whose variance is smallest (signal in the noise directions of X). Show ridge (which damps low-variance directions) underperforms OLS-with-selectkbest or PLS here, and quantify the gap by CV. This is the “ridge fails when variance ≠ relevance” experiment — the exact motivation for supervised direction methods, and the reason 5.5 exists.

7. Summary

  • Ridge = least squares + λ‖β‖²; closed form \((X^\top X + \lambda I)^{-1}X^\top y\); λ > 0 rescues singular problems.
  • Bias for variance: paths shrink smoothly, never to zero; λ interpolates OLS → mean-model.
  • Standardize first; leave the intercept unpenalized; tune α by CV inside a pipeline; nested CV for claims.
  • Shrunken coefficients are parameters, not effect sizes — no naive p-values.
  • SVD view: each direction damped by \(s_i^2/(s_i^2+\lambda)\) — soft, stable, blind to direction relevance.
  • Collinear groups share credit smoothly — honest under duplication, unlike coin-flip subset selection.
  • Shrink-to-zero is often what you actually want — sparsity next: 5.3 Lasso regression.
