0.4 Visualisation & Exploratory Analysis

Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) is the discipline of interrogating data before modeling: distributions first, relationships second, anomalies always. matplotlib is the plotting engine under nearly every Python plotting library (pandas plotting calls it, seaborn wraps it), so we drill it directly.

Tier 1 — every cell below runs live. Re-plot, don’t just read plots.

1. Basic Concept

matplotlib has two layers: the pyplot state machine (plt.plot(...)) for quick plots, and the object-oriented interface (fig, ax = plt.subplots(); then ax.plot(...)) which is the right habit for anything non-trivial. Anatomy you should be able to name: Figure (the canvas), Axes (one plot panel), axis (with limits, ticks, labels), title, legend.

The three plots that carry 90% of EDA:

EDA is a loop, not a plot. The minimal protocol you should run on any new table:

  1. .head(), .shape, .dtypes — what is this, actually?
  2. .describe() and .isna().sum() — ranges, centers, holes.
  3. Histogram of every numeric column — skew, multimodality, impossible values (negative ages).
  4. Scatter/correlation for relationships you care about.
  5. Boxplots by group — does grouping explain spread?

2. Concept Questions

  1. In fig, ax = plt.subplots(), what is the type and role of ax?
  2. Your histogram has one tall bar at 0 and a flat sea of bars to the right. What transform should you almost certainly try?
  3. Why is alpha=0.3 or smaller essential when scatter-plotting thousands of points?
  4. What does a boxplot’s whisker-cap convention actually mark, and what is plotted as individual points?
  5. Name two questions a groupby + boxplot answers that describe() alone cannot.
Answers
  1. ax is an Axes object — one panel. All plotting methods hang off it (ax.hist, ax.set_xlabel); the OO interface avoids pyplot’s hidden global state and is required for multi-panel figures.
  2. A log transform (np.log10 / np.log1p). Mean ≫ median in .describe() is the numeric tell; a spike at zero with a long right tail is the visual tell.
  3. Overplotting: with thousands of points, opaque markers hide density — the plot then shows only where points exist, not where they concentrate. Alpha (transparency) makes density visible as darkness.
  4. Whiskers mark the most extreme data points within 1.5×IQR of the quartiles; points beyond are drawn individually as (candidate) outliers. The fence is a convention, not a definition of “outlier.”
  5. Whether distributions differ in shape (not just center) between groups, and whether sample sizes are comparable (a tiny group’s mean is noise). describe() pools everything.

3. Drills & Exercises

Drills

Small plotting reflexes. For each: predict the shape of the output, run, compare.

Exercises (with solutions)

Exercise 1. Full EDA pass on the simulated cohort in one figure: (a) 2×2 grid with histograms of age, log10(marker), a scatter of age vs log-marker, and a boxplot of log-marker by group. Every panel titled and every axis labeled.

Solution
Note the log transform before plotting the skewed marker — the histogram becomes readable and the boxplot comparison becomes fair.

Exercise 2. Demonstrate bin-width sensitivity: plot the same 200-point sample as 5, 20, and 100-bin histograms side by side. Write one sentence on what the “right” number of bins depends on.

Solution
There is no “right” bin count — it depends on sample size and the question (rough guides: Sturges 1+log2(n), Freedman–Diaconis based on IQR). With n=200, 100 bins mostly display sampling noise; 5 bins can hide bimodality. Look at several.

Exercise 3. Build a scatter matrix (pairplot by hand): for the 3 numeric columns of the cohort, produce a 3×3 grid, diagonal = histograms, off-diagonal = scatter. No seaborn.

Solution
This double loop is the whole idea of a pairs plot (R: GGally::ggpairs). You will later recognize PCA and clustering as ways to make such grids interpretable in high dimensions.

4. Advanced Information & Pitfalls

  • Y-axis truncation exaggerates differences. Bar charts starting at non-zero are a classic misleading visual (and a classic reviewer complaint). For line charts truncation is fine; for bars, don’t.
  • Dual axes are a lie amplifier. Two independent y-scales let you manufacture any “correlation.” Prefer two stacked panels sharing an x-axis.
  • Log scales with zeros/negatives break (log(0) = -inf): use symlog or transform with np.log1p after checking for non-positives.
  • Histograms are model statements: bins impose structure that may not exist (bimodality can appear/vanish with bin width — Exercise 2).
  • Correlation plots lie under nonlinearity: r ≈ 0 says “no linear relation,” nothing more (see 1.2 Relationships).
  • Colorblind-safe palettes (tab10, viridis) and direct labeling beat red-green pairs and remote legends.
  • matplotlib in Pyodide: always end with plt.show(); set fig-width/fig-height cell options (inches) rather than figsize when you want consistent embedding.

5. Advanced Questions

Q1. A colleague says: “the histogram is bimodal, so there are two subpopulations.” Give three alternative explanations and describe the EDA you would run to distinguish them.

Hint Think: sampling noise (Exercise 2), mixtures of process regimes (batch), and transform artifacts.
Solution
  1. Sampling noise: with small n, bin counts fluctuate into a fake second mode — re-draw with different bin widths/seeds (Exercise 2 logic) and bootstrap the histogram. (b) Batch/technical structure: data collected in two runs, sites, or instruments — color the histogram by batch; check within-batch shape; a two-sample test across the suspected split (1.4 Statistical inference) should vanish within batch. (c) Transform artifact: e.g. log of a mixture of a point-mass at zero plus positives, or periodic rounding (integer counts). Plot untransformed, check value granularity. Also consider a genuine mixture — but the burden of proof is after (a)–(c) are excluded: overlay a fitted two-component normal mixture and check the gap depth.

Q2. Explain why ax.set_yscale("log") often “fixes” a scatter where the vertical spread grows with x, and what that visual pattern implies for modeling choices later (name the variance structure).

Solution Funnel-shaped residuals — spread proportional to the mean level — indicate multiplicative noise: y = f(x)·ε rather than y = f(x) + ε. Multiplicative error becomes additive and roughly homoscedastic after a log transform, which is why log axes tidy such plots. Modeling consequence: fit the model to log(y) (or use a GLM with a log link and variance proportional to mean², gamma/Poisson-style), rather than vanilla least squares which weights the large-y points overwhelmingly. The EDA observation is literally a specification diagnostic for Part 2 regression.

6. Complex Questions

C1 (EDA × 0.3 pandas). Using groupby + the plotting drills, construct the “small multiples” figure: one histogram per group on a shared x-range. Which two pandas/matplotlib mechanics make shared scales essential for honest comparison, and what does each group’s count tell you about how much to trust each panel?

C2 (EDA × 1.1 Descriptive statistics). Generate lognormal data, then plot mean and median of increasing samples (n = 5, 10, 50, 200, 1000, each resampled many times) as dot strips. Which estimator’s strip narrows faster, and how does the histogram shape you saw in Section 1 predict that result? Connect: skew → sampling distribution of the mean vs median → why “median for skewed data” is a precision claim, not just an aesthetic one.

7. Summary

  • EDA protocol: head/dtypes → describe/isna → distributions → relationships → by-group comparisons.
  • Use the OO interface (fig, ax = plt.subplots()); label everything; end cells with plt.show().
  • Alpha and jitter reveal density; truncated bar axes and dual y-axes manufacture stories.
  • Transform skewed data (log) before comparing and plotting — mean ≫ median is the flag.
  • Bin width is an assumption; look at several before claiming structure.
  • Boxplots compare distributions; scatter matrices compare everything pairwise.
  • Pictures suggest; only inference decides — next page sets up the train/test discipline that makes model evaluation honest: 0.5 Data splitting.
